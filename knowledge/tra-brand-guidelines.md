# Tax Relief Advocates (TRA) — Brand Guidelines

## Logo

The supplied TRA logo image is the single source of truth for the logo.

- Use the actual supplied logo asset in final creative. Do not ask an image model to redraw, regenerate, approximate, reinterpret, or typeset the TRA logo.
- Preserve the logo exactly: wording, typography, spacing, proportions, colors, and internal layout must remain unchanged.
- Proportional resizing and placement are allowed. Do not crop, stretch, recolor, simplify, or reconstruct the logo.
- If an image-generation model cannot reproduce the logo exactly, generate the design without relying on the model-rendered logo and composite the original TRA logo asset into the final creative afterward.
- A near-match, stylized version, or text recreation is not an acceptable substitute for the supplied logo.

## Brand Colors

- `#0577BF`
- `#6D6E71`
- `#FFFFFF`
- `#333333`

Use these colors as the primary TRA palette, but preserve the visual logic of a strong reference creative when strict use of every brand color would make the design less effective.

## Voice & Tone

TRA’s voice and tone should be:

- Clear
- Professional
- Reassuring
- Patient
- Straightforward
- Approachable
- Empathetic
- No-pressure

## Visual Style

TRA creative should feel professionally art-directed, restrained, and intentional. Preserve the strongest design logic of the reference creative rather than filling available space with extra marketing elements.

The supplied brand and website assets include the TRA logo, service-step icons, lock imagery, client imagery, and review-related assets. These are optional resources, not a checklist for every ad.

- Do not automatically add a benefits section, benefit icons, feature rows, trust badges, floating cards, extra text boxes, CTA bars, proof blocks, or decorative elements.
- Only introduce a secondary section when it is important to the reference concept or necessary to communicate the specific ad idea.
- If the reference is visually simple, keep the TRA adaptation visually simple.
- Favor one clear focal idea, strong hierarchy, deliberate whitespace, and clean image-to-text balance over information density.
- Avoid making every TRA ad follow the same template. The reference creative should guide composition, hierarchy, spacing, image treatment, and overall visual mechanism.

## Copy Style

Use plain, clear consumer language.

- Explain things directly.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Favor straightforward headlines and calls to action over hype.
- Match the copy density of the reference when practical; do not add supporting copy merely to fill space.
- Keep secondary copy subordinate to the primary idea.
